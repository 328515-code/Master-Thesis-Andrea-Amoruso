\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis investigated the hydraulic response of four excess-flow valve obturators for hydrogen refueling applications and translated the resulting CFD data into an interactive design tool.\\

The study, as seen, considered a fixed obturator position at a reference state of $437\,\mathrm{bar}$ absolute and $233\,\mathrm{K}$, with Hydrogen and as secondary option Nitrogen. The 24-case factorial campaign combined four different valve geometries and three inlet velocities for each gas. OpenFOAM calculations, consistent post-processing and documented data reduction provided a common basis for comparing pressure loss and axial loading.\\

The principal result is the persistent ranking $A>C>B>D$ for both pressure loss and positive-$x$ force at every investigated inlet velocity. Smaller total passage areas increase acceleration through the obturator and the associated hydraulic resistance and loading. For hydrogen at $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, configuration A gives approximately $297.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $50.09\,\mathrm{N}$, compared with $15.55\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $1.53\,\mathrm{N}$ for D. The contour analysis supported this interpretation by showing the strongest passage acceleration and pressure contrasts in the more restrictive geometries. These results identify a design trade-off: a larger fluid-induced load may assist closure, but it also carries a greater pressure penalty during normal operation.\\

Nitrogen produces substantially larger dimensional loads at equal inlet velocity, principally because its prescribed density is about 14.25 times that of hydrogen. With normalization, the pressure-loss and force coefficients are much closer and retain the same geometric ordering. The comparison supports approximately quadratic response trends within the investigated conditions; it does not establish gas interchangeability. In particular, equal inlet velocity corresponds to different mass flows for the two gases.\\

The completed Streamlit application makes these results accessible through two inputs: gas and target mass flow. For each obturator, a quadratic least-squares fit converts the selected flow into an estimated axial force and pressure loss, while the original CFD points remain visible, distinguishing the sampled interval from extrapolation and marking a geometrical estimate of the local $Mach <0.3$ condition. This provides a practical connection between the simulation campaign and preliminary spring load selection, while retaining the assumptions behind the numerical prediction.\\

The local Mach assessment is especially relevant to nitrogen, since the largest reference Mach number is approximately $0.107$ for Hydrogen and $0.298$ for Nitrogen, both for configuration A at the highest inlet velocity. The tool projects the largest observed velocity amplification ratio for each gas and geometry, rather than treating the inlet Mach number as representative of every passage, creating a screening criterion.\\

Hydrogen simulations with obturator B require particular care because its force variability and fit mismatch are larger than those of the other configurations, even if the check of its results (residuals stabilization, mesh quality issues, local Mach number, obturator geometry) did not highlight particular issues.\\

It is important to underline that the force value obtained from the study cannot determine spring stiffness alone since it is at one fixed position. The designer should also keep in mind the closure time or a certified trip setting, preload, stroke, friction and the displacement dependent fluid load.\\

Further development should therefore prioritize numerical refinement of the most sensitive cases and additional flow points near the intended operating threshold. Moreover, simulations at several obturator positions, followed by coupled motion and experimental pressure-loss and trip tests, would connect the present loading estimates to actual valve operation. However this work, within its limitations, still offers a traceable comparison of candidate geometries using a starting case fine-tuned to run without issues many conditions without the necessity of changing simulation parameters or increasing the computational demand, thereby fulfilling the industrial objective of the study. Moreover, the final tool allows users to consult the results comparison, without requiring direct use of CFD solver and post processing software,  providing an accessible quantitative starting point for the subsequent mechanical design of the EFV.
