\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis develops a structured CFD-based methodology for investigating excess-flow valve (EFV) obturators intended for hydrogen-vehicle refuelling stations. The industrial installation point is downstream of the pre-cooler, close to the dispenser, where operation at high pressure and low temperature is of direct engineering relevance. The final CFD campaign considers a nominal reference state of $437\,\mathrm{bar}$ and $233\,\mathrm{K}$ (approximately $-40\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$). This choice is consistent with the pre-cooled dispensing context addressed by SAE~J2601, while the safety performance of EFVs is within the scope of ISO~19880-3 \cite{sae_j2601,iso19880_3}.

\paragraph{Computational framework.}
The numerical procedure combines parametric obturator geometries, OpenFOAM~10 finite-volume calculations, automated meshing and case preparation, parallel execution on ARIES HPC infrastructure and consistent reduction of pressure and force histories. The full-factorial Design of Experiments varies three inlet velocities ($5$, $10$, and $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$), two hole counts, two hole diameters, and two working gases. The central objective remains the behaviour of \emph{hydrogen}, the actual refuelling fuel. Nitrogen is included only as an industrially relevant complementary gas for comparing the consequences of different density and viscosity, not as a physically equivalent replacement for hydrogen.

\paragraph{Principal hydrogen findings.}
In the final high-pressure simulations, more restrictive obturator passages generate both greater pressure loss and a greater positive-$x$ hydrodynamic force. At $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, obturator A produces an averaged pressure difference of approximately $297.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and axial force of $50.09\,\mathrm{N}$, whereas obturator D gives approximately $15.55\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $1.53\,\mathrm{N}$. For each inlet velocity, both hydraulic responses follow the same geometric ranking from largest to smallest: A, C, B, D. These results demonstrate a trade-off between maintaining a low hydraulic penalty during normal operation and developing a fluid-induced load capable of contributing to mechanical EFV closure.

\paragraph{Secondary nitrogen interpretation.}
The nitrogen results generally show much larger dimensional pressure differences and forces than hydrogen at equal inlet velocity, reflecting the significantly greater reference density and the different Reynolds number. These are \emph{equal-velocity}, not equal-mass-flow, comparisons. A source-data duplication was identified in the nitrogen obturator-A result at $5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; that response is therefore excluded from the independently interpreted nitrogen results. The remaining nitrogen comparisons provide additional context but do not alter the hydrogen-centred application objective.

\paragraph{Numerical scope and engineering interpretation.}
The force and pressure statistics are averages over the final 301 solver iterations. The hydrogen obturator-B cases at the higher velocities exhibit appreciable terminal-window fluctuations, and high local $y^+$ values demand care in interpreting the near-wall predictions. Furthermore, the fixed-obturator, constant-density model predicts the hydraulic loading at prescribed geometry; it does not calculate actual obturator motion, the complete valve trip threshold or the transient filling and rupture processes. The numerical responses should therefore be used as design-comparison data within the documented modelling assumptions, rather than as direct evidence of component certification \cite{moukalled2016,iso19880_3}.

\paragraph{Final engineering deliverable.}
The concluding development stage concerns implementation of the executable valve-design tool, which will present hydrogen pressure-loss and force curves as the primary comparison and nitrogen as a secondary option. By retaining case properties, response definitions, averaging intervals, force signs and numerical-quality flags, the application can translate the CFD study into an inspectable engineering comparison. The computational simulations themselves constitute the completed simulation campaign; the future work is focused on the user interface and associated design-selection logic, together with any later verification required for industrial qualification.
