\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

The work presented in this thesis develops a computational framework for evaluating the flow behaviour of excess-flow valves intended for hydrogen refuelling systems. The engineering problem is to reconcile two competing requirements: limiting the pressure loss during normal dispensing and providing sufficient hydrodynamic load to support a mechanical excess-flow response. EFVs form one part of the protective arrangements considered in standards for gaseous hydrogen fuelling stations, such as ISO~19880-1 and ISO~19880-3 \cite{iso19880_1,iso19880_3}.

\paragraph{Numerical and methodological contributions.}
The computational procedure integrates CAD-based valve geometry, structured background meshing, surface-aware refinement, steady incompressible flow calculations with OpenFOAM 10, parallel execution on the ARIES HPC system and automated processing of the resulting pressure, flux and force histories. The full-factorial Design of Experiments varies two working gases, three inlet velocities and four obturator geometries constructed from combinations of hole count and diameter. This makes the comparison repeatable and helps separate geometrical effects from changes in operating conditions.

\paragraph{Fluid-dynamic response.}
The hydrogen calculations demonstrate a marked trade-off between restriction and obturator loading. At $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the averaged pressure difference and positive-$x$ hydrodynamic force for obturator A are approximately $980\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $0.1634\,\mathrm{N}$; for obturator D, the corresponding values are approximately $52.5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $0.00412\,\mathrm{N}$. Across the investigated hydrogen inlet velocities, these responses increase as flow velocity increases and differ substantially among the four geometries. The results quantify the loading on a fixed obturator, while the closing threshold of an actual valve also depends on preload, spring stiffness, component displacement and the orientation of motion.

\paragraph{Relevance to hydrogen refuelling.}
The study supplies a fluid-dynamic foundation for comparing EFV configurations within the broader engineering task of designing high-pressure hydrogen dispensing equipment. The refuelling environment brings variable pressure, temperature and flow demands, and the SAE~J2601 protocol defines operating constraints for vehicle filling \cite{sae_j2601}. The numerical reference conditions must therefore be distinguished from the full station operating envelope. Extension to further thermodynamic conditions requires consistent real-fluid properties and, where appropriate, assessment of local compressibility.

\paragraph{Further development.}
The comparative data-processing framework can support an interactive engineering tool that communicates pressure-loss and axial-force trends with explicit units and provenance. Future extensions may include additional operating states, refinement-sensitivity studies, mechanical force-balance evaluation and transient or coupled fluid--structure analyses. These developments connect the CFD assessment of candidate geometries to component-level design and qualification procedures for hydrogen service \cite{iso19880_3}.
