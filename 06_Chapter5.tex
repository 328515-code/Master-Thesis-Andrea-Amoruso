\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This work describes a reproducible CFD-based framework for comparing excess flow valve obturators using a full-factorial Design of Experiments (DoE). The reference simulation campaign comprises two working gases ($\mathrm{H}_2$ and $\mathrm{N}_2$), three imposed inlet velocities ($5$, $10$ and $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$), and four geometries defined by combinations of three or six holes with diameters of $1.5$ or $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The workflow integrates OpenFOAM 10, automated case preparation, an HPC execution pipeline and post-processing reports.

\paragraph{Available numerical evidence.}
The computational study defines all 24 case configurations and provides pressure, force, volume-flux and wall-distance histories for the 12 hydrogen cases. The nitrogen setups are defined, but their numerical \texttt{postProcessing} results are not yet available. The two gases have been configured with the reference-property values described in Chapter~\ref{ch:doe}; these are not high-pressure, low-temperature calculations.

\paragraph{Preliminary hydrogen findings.}
Over the last 301 solver iterations, the hydrogen results show increasing pressure drop and positive-$x$ obturator force with increasing inlet velocity for each geometry. In all three velocity conditions, the preliminary ordering of pressure drop and axial force is A, C, B and D. At $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the terminal-window averages for obturator A are approximately $980\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $0.1634\,\mathrm{N}$, compared with $52.5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $0.00412\,\mathrm{N}$ for obturator D. These outcomes suggest a design trade-off between reduced flow resistance and increased hydrodynamic loading.

\paragraph{Remaining limitations.}
All twelve hydrogen histories contain finite values through solver iteration 3000, but neither final residual convergence nor grid independence has been established from the available numerical results. An outlet flow-rate diagnostic and the nitrogen results are also missing. Consequently, the observations are preliminary and cannot support a completed fluid-to-fluid comparison or a validated valve ranking. Furthermore, a steady incompressible simulation with a fixed obturator does not resolve transient closing motion or a valve trip threshold.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
Future work should verify the hydrogen solutions, obtain and assess nitrogen post-processing data, complete the factorial comparisons and implement an interactive tool that exposes its data-quality and interpolation limits. Additional real-fluid modeling and possible compressible simulations would be necessary to evaluate the separately discussed operating state of $437\,\mathrm{bar}$ and $-40\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.
