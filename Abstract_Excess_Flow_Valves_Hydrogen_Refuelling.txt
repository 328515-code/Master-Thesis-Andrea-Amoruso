This thesis investigates the fluid-dynamic design of excess-flow valves (EFVs) for hydrogen vehicle refuelling systems, focusing on a spring-loaded valve positioned downstream of the pre-cooler, near the dispenser, where hydrogen is delivered at high pressure and low temperature. The operating principle is examined through the balance between hydrodynamic forces and mechanical resistance, highlighting the trade-off between pressure losses during normal flow and the load required to initiate closure under excess-flow conditions. A three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) workflow is developed in OpenFOAM 10, integrating automated case preparation and meshing, high-performance computing (HPC), and systematic post-processing. A full-factorial Design of Experiments (DoE) considers hydrogen and nitrogen, different inlet velocities, and four obturator geometries defined by the number and diameter of the holes. Reference-state simulations are used to investigate how geometric restriction influences the pressure drop and axial hydrodynamic force, while high-pressure, low-temperature operation defines the intended refuelling application. The study also supports the development of an executable design tool intended to help engineers compare valve configurations and select suitable candidates using CFD-derived performance indicators. The resulting methodology provides a traceable basis for engineering assessment while distinguishing fixed-obturator, steady incompressible predictions from the transient mechanical closure of an operating valve.
