This thesis investigates the fluid-dynamic design of excess-flow valves (EFVs) for hydrogen vehicle refuelling systems, focusing on a valve positioned downstream of the pre-cooler, near the dispenser, using a high-pressure, low-temperature fluid.
The operating principle of a spring-loaded EFV is examined through the balance between hydrodynamic forces and mechanical resistance, linking normal-flow pressure losses to the tendency towards closure.
A three-dimensional CFD workflow is developed in OpenFOAM 10, with automated meshing, high-performance computing (HPC) and subsequent post-processing, the analysis carried uses a full-factorial Design of Experiments considering: hydrogen and nitrogen as operational fluids, different inlet velocities, and four obturator geometries defined by hole number and diameter.
Reference-state simulations illustrate how geometric restriction influences the pressure drop and axial fluid load developing also an executable tool that aids the designer in the valve choice.
The work ends by outlining a traceable framework, comparing valve concepts, including the limitations of fixed-obturator, incompressible simulations.
