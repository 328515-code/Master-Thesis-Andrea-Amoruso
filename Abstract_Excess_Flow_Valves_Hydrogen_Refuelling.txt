This thesis investigates the fluid-dynamic design of excess-flow valves for hydrogen refuelling systems, with emphasis on the relationship between flow restriction, pressure loss and the load acting on a valve obturator. The working principle of spring-loaded excess-flow valves is examined in the context of hydrogen dispensing and its safety requirements. A three-dimensional computational methodology is developed in OpenFOAM 10, combining geometry-specific meshing, steady incompressible flow simulations and high-performance computing automation. A full-factorial Design of Experiments considers hydrogen and nitrogen, three inlet velocities (5, 10 and 15 m/s), two hole counts and two hole diameters, yielding 24 configurations. Automated post-processing extracts the pressure difference, inlet flow rate and axial obturator force for systematic comparison. The hydrogen results show that more restrictive geometries generate larger pressure losses and higher hydrodynamic loads, highlighting the trade-off relevant to valve design. The work also outlines an interactive tool for comparing candidate configurations and identifies the distinction between fixed-obturator CFD predictions and the complete mechanical closing response under refuelling conditions.
