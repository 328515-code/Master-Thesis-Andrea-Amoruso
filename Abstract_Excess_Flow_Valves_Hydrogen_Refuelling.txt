This thesis investigates the fluid-dynamic design of excess-flow valves (EFVs) for hydrogen vehicle refuelling systems, focusing on a spring loaded valve positioned downstream of the pre-cooler. The operating principle is examined through the balance between hydrodynamic forces and mechanical resistance, highlighting the trade-off between pressure loss during normal flow and the load acting on the obturator under excess-flow conditions.
A three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) methodology is developed in OpenFOAM 10, integrating automated case preparation, meshing, high-performance computing and systematic post-processing. A full-factorial Design of Experiments considers three inlet velocities and four obturator geometries at a nominal reference condition of 437 bar and 233 K. Hydrogen is the primary working fluid, reflecting the intended refuelling application; nitrogen is included as a secondary industrial gas comparison.
For hydrogen, the simulations show that more restrictive passages produce greater pressure losses and axial forces. The results establish a quantitative basis for comparing candidate valve geometries. The final engineering application will be an executable design tool using CFD-derived performance indicators. The study distinguishes fixed-obturator, steady incompressible predictions from the transient mechanical closure of an operating valve.
