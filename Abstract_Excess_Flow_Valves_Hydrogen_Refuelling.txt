This thesis investigates the fluid-dynamic design of excess-flow valves (EFVs) for hydrogen vehicle refuelling systems, focusing on a valve positioned downstream of the pre-cooler, near the dispenser. The intended application motivates a high-pressure, low-temperature reference state of approximately 430 bar absolute and -40 °C. The operating principle of a spring-loaded EFV is examined through the balance between hydrodynamic forces and mechanical resistance, linking normal-flow pressure losses to the tendency towards closure. A three-dimensional CFD workflow is developed in OpenFOAM 10, with automated meshing, high-performance computing and post-processing. A full-factorial Design of Experiments considers hydrogen and nitrogen, inlet velocities of 5, 10 and 15 m/s, and four obturator geometries defined by hole number and diameter. Reference-state simulations illustrate how geometric restriction influences the pressure drop and axial fluid load. The study distinguishes these baseline findings from application-oriented high-pressure conditions and outlines a traceable framework for comparing valve concepts, including the limitations of fixed-obturator, incompressible simulations.
