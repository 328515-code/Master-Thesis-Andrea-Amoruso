\chapter{Design of Experiments (DoE) and HPC Automation}
\label{ch:doe}

This chapter defines the parameter space of the numerical Design of Experiments (DoE) and the workflow for producing, running and organizing the cases. The reference configuration is the \texttt{SIM\_DoE\_2210} campaign. The 24 combinations define the factorial design. The uploaded archive contains all 24 case configurations and 12 hydrogen result histories, but no nitrogen \texttt{postProcessing} histories. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} therefore reports preliminary hydrogen responses and keeps the full two-fluid comparison pending numerical verification and receipt of nitrogen outputs.

\section{Definition of the DoE Parameter Space}
\label{sec:doe_parameters}

The four physical factors are working fluid, inlet velocity, number of obturator holes and diameter of each hole. Their selected levels are listed in Table~\ref{tab:doe_factors}. The resulting full factorial design comprises
\begin{equation}
    N_{\mathrm{cases}}=2_{\mathrm{fluids}}\times3_{\mathrm{velocities}}\times
    2_{\mathrm{hole\ counts}}\times2_{\mathrm{diameters}}=24.
    \label{eq:doe_cases}
\end{equation}
Treating hole count and diameter as separate factors makes it possible to discuss their individual contributions and their interaction rather than considering geometry only as an unstructured four-level category.

\subsection{Working Fluids ($\mathrm{H}_2$ and $\mathrm{N}_2$)}
Hydrogen and nitrogen were selected to investigate gases with markedly different density and viscosity. The values used in the incompressible cases, reported at the reference state $T=20\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $p=1\,\mathrm{atm}$, are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:fluid_properties} \cite{nist_refprop}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Reference fluid properties used to define the DoE. The density is also needed when converting kinematic pressure or scaling forces consistently.}
\label{tab:fluid_properties}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Gas} & $\rho$ [$\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$] & $\mu$ [$\mathrm{Pa\,s}$] & $\nu$ [$\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$]\\
\midrule
$\mathrm{H}_2$ & $0.0838$ & $8.80\times10^{-6}$ & $1.05\times10^{-4}$\\
$\mathrm{N}_2$ & $1.165$ & $1.76\times10^{-5}$ & $1.51\times10^{-5}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The incompressible momentum solution depends on kinematic viscosity, and thus on the Reynolds number $Re=U_{\mathrm{in}}D_{\mathrm{in}}/\nu$. At equal inlet velocity and geometry, a comparison of the two gases changes this Reynolds number. Dimensional pressure losses and forces also depend on density, so a cross-fluid comparison must distinguish the kinematic flow response from its dimensional scaling. The atmospheric reference state is a modeling convention: the uploaded dictionaries use $\nu_{\mathrm{H}_2}=1.05\times10^{-4}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{N}_2}=1.51\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$, while the force dictionaries use $\rho_{\mathrm{H}_2}=0.0838$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{N}_2}=1.165\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Because \texttt{simpleFoam} solves a kinematic-pressure field with a relative pressure datum, an absolute pressure of $1\,\mathrm{atm}$ is not explicitly enforced through the \texttt{0/p} outlet value. The gas-property set, rather than the absolute pressure datum, defines the reference-state approximation. The separately considered operating condition of $437\,\mathrm{bar}$ and $-40\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is not represented by the present files.

\subsection{Inlet Velocity Levels and Reference Flow Rates}
The three imposed inlet velocities in \texttt{SIM\_DoE\_2210} are $5$, $10$ and $15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. They define a low-to-moderate inlet-velocity range for a systematic comparison; they are not identified here with verified nominal, surge or line-rupture trip conditions. Any association with a particular valve specification must be supported by the corresponding operating requirements.

The nominal circular inlet diameter is $D_{\mathrm{in}}=7.8\,\mathrm{mm}$, giving
\begin{equation}
    A_{\mathrm{in}}=\pi\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{in}}}{2}\right)^2
    =\pi(0.0039\,\mathrm{m})^2\simeq4.778\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
    \label{eq:inlet_area}
\end{equation}
For a prescribed uniform inlet velocity, the corresponding nominal volumetric and reference mass-flow rates are
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{nominal}}=U_{\mathrm{in}}A_{\mathrm{in}},
    \qquad
    \dot m_{\mathrm{ref}}=\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}Q_{\mathrm{nominal}}.
    \label{eq:inlet_flow}
\end{equation}
The quantities in Table~\ref{tab:flow_regimes} use the nominal CAD inlet area and reference densities, not a direct integral over the final discretized inlet patch. The latter should be used when checking the simulated flux.

The corresponding inlet Reynolds numbers are approximately $371$, $743$ and $1114$ for hydrogen, compared with $2583$, $5166$ and $7748$ for nitrogen. These are inlet-scale values and do not establish whether the separated flow around the obturator is everywhere laminar or turbulent. Likewise, the inlet Mach number is insufficient to certify incompressibility within the more restrictive passages (Section~\ref{sec:governing_equations}).

\subsection{Obturator Geometries (\texttt{wallObt})}
Two levels of hole count and two levels of hole diameter define the four CAD variants. With the nominal total hole area
\begin{equation}
    A_{\mathrm{holes}}=n\pi\frac{d^2}{4},
    \qquad
    \beta=\frac{A_{\mathrm{holes}}}{A_{\mathrm{in}}},
    \label{eq:open_area_ratio}
\end{equation}
the open-area ratio $\beta$ quantifies the nominal restriction relative to the inlet. It represents the sum of the specified circular hole areas and is not necessarily the complete effective flow area if other bypass passages exist.

The open-area ratio provides a useful geometric descriptor, but local contraction, the obturator shape and recirculation may also affect the pressure drop and axial force. Consequently, the relative performance of the four variants is a hypothesis to be examined from verified CFD outputs rather than assumed in advance.

\section{Python Case Generation and Submission Pipeline}
\label{sec:python_doe_generator}

A Python-based workflow is used to reduce repetitive manual case preparation. Its intended operations are to copy a reference OpenFOAM case, insert the selected viscosity and inlet velocity, replace the \texttt{wallObt.stl} geometry and assign a unique directory name to each factor combination. Cases can then be transferred to the HPC system and submitted with the relevant SLURM execution script.

An illustrative generator, updated to the levels and active gas properties of the supplied campaign, is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_doe_gen}. Because the original template and the historical generator invocation were not included in the new archive, the listing is documented as a reproducible reconstruction rather than the exact script that launched the original jobs. The factorial design in Table~\ref{tab:doe_factors}, not the outdated velocity list in that provisional appendix, defines the reference study in this chapter.

For reproducibility, the final report should record the exact parameter dictionary and geometry file associated with each generated case, the final scripts and the solver version. It should also differentiate the number of cases \emph{generated or submitted} from the number that meet the numerical acceptance criteria.

\section{Automated Remote Post-Processing Extraction}
\label{sec:scp_extraction}

The OpenFOAM run directories include processor meshes and field files that are substantially larger than the reduced \texttt{postProcessing/} outputs used for preliminary response screening. A local Python script can retrieve each case's \texttt{postProcessing/} directory using \texttt{scp} while preserving the simulation's directory identifier, thereby reducing unnecessary transfer and the risk of mixing cases.

The example in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_scp_script} uses a project-specific remote-path placeholder because the present archive does not establish the original cluster storage path. The uploaded folders contain hydrogen \texttt{postProcessing} data but only setup files for nitrogen. Retrieved data are not automatically accepted as valid steady-state responses. Force, pressure and flux histories must first pass the checks introduced in Section~\ref{sec:numerical_verification}.

\section{Response Variables and Planned DoE Analysis}
\label{sec:doe_analysis}

The principal outputs are the inlet-to-outlet pressure difference, the total axial force on the stationary obturator and the inlet flow rate. For an accepted steady solution, one may define
\begin{equation}
    \Delta p=\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}\left(P_{\mathrm{in}}-P_{\mathrm{out}}\right),
    \qquad
    F_x=F_{p,x}+F_{\nu,x},
    \label{eq:doe_responses}
\end{equation}
where $P$ is kinematic pressure. In the submitted hydrogen \texttt{forces.dat} files, $F_x$ has already been dimensionalized with \texttt{rhoInf = 0.0838}; no additional density multiplication is applied. The sign of $F_x$ is defined relative to the positive streamwise $x$ axis; it must not automatically be identified as a positive closing force without confirming the mechanism's actual direction of travel.

For comparisons across flow levels and gases, the following coefficients are proposed:
\begin{equation}
    K=\frac{\Delta p}{\tfrac12\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}U_{\mathrm{in}}^2},
    \qquad
    C_{F,x}=\frac{F_x}{\tfrac12\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}U_{\mathrm{in}}^2 A_{\mathrm{in}}}.
    \label{eq:response_coefficients}
\end{equation}
Here $A_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the selected reference area and must be used consistently in all cases. Comparisons should include main effects for gas, velocity, hole count and hole diameter and selected interaction plots, particularly hole count $\times$ hole diameter and fluid $\times$ velocity.

Because these are deterministic numerical runs without independent physical replicates, an apparent statistical fit must not be interpreted as experimental uncertainty. Any regression, factorial contrast or variance-based analysis should be accompanied by numerical-quality checks and the limits of the chosen sample. No quantitative ranking or predictive interpolation is asserted until a sufficiently complete and accepted response table is available.
