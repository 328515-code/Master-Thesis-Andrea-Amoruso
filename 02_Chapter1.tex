\chapter{Numerical Model and CFD Setup}
\label{ch:numerical_setup}

This chapter describes the mathematical model, computational domain, boundary conditions, discretization and HPC workflow adopted for the excess flow valve simulations. Since several cases are still undergoing numerical stabilization, the present chapter distinguishes the \emph{specified} setup from verification results that have yet to be established.

\section{Governing Equations and Modeling Assumptions}
\label{sec:governing_equations}

The numerical model represents three-dimensional, steady, single-phase flow around a fixed obturator. The OpenFOAM 10 solver \texttt{simpleFoam} solves an incompressible, constant-density Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) system \cite{openfoam_user,moukalled2016}. \\
Neglecting body forces, the mass-conservation equation is
\begin{equation}
    \nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}=0,
    \label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{U}$ denotes mean velocity. \\
Instead, a schematic eddy-viscosity representation of the mean momentum balance is
\begin{equation}
    \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U})
    =-\nabla P+\nabla\cdot\left[(\nu+\nu_t)
    \left(\nabla\mathbf{U}+(\nabla\mathbf{U})^{T}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:momentum}
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the kinematic pressure, $\nu=\mu/\rho$ is the molecular kinematic viscosity and $\nu_t$ is the turbulent eddy viscosity. Here the isotropic part of the modeled Reynolds stress is understood to be incorporated into the modified pressure; the equation summarizes the physical closure rather than reproducing every discrete term implemented by \texttt{simpleFoam} \cite{menter1994,openfoam_user}. The solved pressure field has dimensions of $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$; conversion to a pressure difference in pascals requires multiplication by an appropriate density.

The \texttt{constant/momentumTransport} dictionary selects a RANS formulation with the $k$--$\omega$ Shear Stress Transport (SST) model \cite{menter1994}. 

This choice, with its near-wall $k$--$\omega$ behavior and outer-flow $k$--$\epsilon$ blending make it the best solution for flows featuring adverse pressure gradients and separation without a much higher computational cost, relavtive to a standard  $k$--$\omega$. However, this choice does not, by itself, guarantee accuracy.\\
In particular, some operating points have relatively low inlet Reynolds numbers, which can lead to numerical errors in the solution, requiring further convergence checks of the main parameters.\\

The working-fluid kinematic viscosity is defined in \texttt{constant/physicalProperties} using the constant-viscosity model:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=C++,caption={Parameterized constant-viscosity entry used in the OpenFOAM setup.}]
viscosityModel constant;
nu [0 2 -1 0 0 0 0] NU_VALUE;
\end{lstlisting}
The template placeholder illustrated here is replaced for each gas as described in Chapter~\ref{ch:doe}. In the supplied completed case dictionaries, the active values are $\nu_{\mathrm{H}_2}=1.05\times10^{-4}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{N}_2}=1.51\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. They correspond to the reference-state properties used in this study. The inlet turbulence fields are initialized with the initial turbulence quantities $k=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$ and $\omega=10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Their physical basis is not asserted here as universally representative.

\subsection*{Assessment of the incompressible approximation}
The common guideline $Ma\lesssim0.3$ refers to the \emph{local} Mach number, $Ma=|\mathbf{U}|/c$, rather than only the inlet value \cite{anderson2017}. Velocity increases within the obturator passages may therefore produce locally a so definable compressible flow even when the inlet velocity is moderate. For illustration, if all inlet flow passed through the nominal holes of obturator A, continuity would give an average passage velocity approximately nine times the inlet velocity; this estimate is not a computed maximum because other flow paths and contraction effects must be examined. The eventual validity assessment should include a local-velocity check against gas sound speed. The incompressible model is treated as a modeling assumption, and will be verified in every simulated scenario.

\section{Computational Domain and Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:boundary_conditions}

The fluid domain is bounded by five principal named surface patches: \texttt{inlet}, \texttt{outlet}, \texttt{wall1}, \texttt{wall2} and \texttt{wallObt}. These names describe boundary regions of a single computational domain, not five independent fluid domains. The stationary obturator is represented by the \texttt{wallObt} surface. The inlet and outlet are positioned away from the most restrictive region to reduce direct interference with the internal flow and interfere with each other.

\section{Numerical Discretization and Execution Workflow}
\label{sec:workflow_automation}

OpenFOAM applies a finite-volume discretization of the steady equations. In the \texttt{fvSchemes} dictionary, velocity convection uses a bounded \texttt{linearUpwindV} scheme with a limited velocity gradient, while the transport of $k$ and $\omega$ uses \texttt{bounded upwind} schemes. Gradient interpolation is mainly linear, and Laplacian terms employ a corrected formulation. These choices allow to reach a balanced resolution and numerical robustness reducing the possibility of non converged solution but still need inspections for oscillations and mesh quality checks.

The Pressure--velocity coupling is based on the SIMPLE algorithm \cite{openfoam_user,moukalled2016}. The \texttt{fvSolution} configuration specifies ten non-orthogonal correction passes and equation relaxation factors of $0.9$ for velocity and $0.5$ for both turbulence variables. The amount of time steps (iterations) used in \texttt{controlDict} is \texttt{3000} with \texttt{deltaT 1}, values chosen after a check on the needed steps for solution convergence, including a small safety margin.

Case execution is managed using Bash scripts that prepare the mesh, partition the domain and launch parallel meshing and solving on ARIES. The principal sequence is \texttt{blockMesh}, \texttt{surfaceFeatures}, \texttt{decomposePar}, parallel \texttt{snappyHexMesh} and parallel \texttt{simpleFoam}. Solver-only restarts are available without repeating the mesh stage. The generation of the complete factorial case set and subsequent retrieval of reduced outputs are addressed in Chapter~\ref{ch:doe}.

\section{Hybrid Mesh Generation Strategy}
\label{sec:mesh_generation}

The computational setup uses a structured hexahedral background grid generated by \texttt{blockMesh}, followed by local castellated refinement, surface snapping and layer generation using \texttt{snappyHexMesh} \cite{openfoam_user}. \\
Initial box extends from $(-0.010,-0.0145,-0.0145)\,\mathrm{m}$ to $(0.175,0.0145,0.0145)\,\mathrm{m}$, generating so $370\times58\times58$ cells, giving $1{,}244{,}680$ \emph{background} cells with nominal spacing $\Delta x=\Delta y=\Delta z=0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. This is not the final snapped-mesh cell count.

The geometry, already shown, has been imported as STL surfaces named \texttt{inlet.stl}, \texttt{outlet.stl}, \texttt{wall1.stl}, \texttt{wall2.stl} and \texttt{wallObt.stl}, where the last one is replaced to generate the different obturator variants. Representative surface statistics for the obturator-A CAD files are provided in Table~\ref{tab:stl_stats}; these counts must not be assumed to be identical for all four obturators.

The \texttt{snappyHexMeshDict} specifies surface refinement level~1 for the inlet and outlet and level~2 for the two wall surfaces and the obturator. It also defines a cylindrical local refinement region near the obturator, with endpoints $(0.035,0,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $(0.046,0,0)\,\mathrm{m}$, radius $0.011\,\mathrm{m}$ and refinement level~2. These are requested refinement settings; the final realized cell distribution must be checked from the generated mesh.

The \texttt{addLayers} option is enabled with an expansion ratio of~2. Layer survival and local near-wall resolution is assessed using the completed mesh and $y^+$ values, asserting that the chosen SST wall treatment is adequately resolved.

\section{HPC Infrastructure and Domain Decomposition}
\label{sec:hpc_environment}

The cases are prepared for execution on the ARIES High-Performance Computing (HPC) infrastructure provided by the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia \cite{hpc_aries}. SLURM is used to manage job allocation; which in this case requests one node and 52 MPI tasks. This is an allocation of MPI ranks and should not be equated with 52 physical cores. The \texttt{scotch} domain decomposition method is specified to distribute cells over the subdomains while limiting communication interfaces \cite{pellegrini1996}.

The number of cells per subdomain and the processor-interface counts depend on the actual mesh and decomposition.

\section{Numerical Verification and Current Limitations}
\label{sec:numerical_verification}

Numerical acceptance is more relevant and important to achieve firstly, rather than reaching the configured final iteration. The following independent checks will be pursued before the simulation data can be considered valid for the final design tool:
\begin{itemize}
    \item residual decay or stabilization together with a stable inlet--outlet pressure difference and axial force history;
    \item conservation of volume or mass flux between inlet and outlet and the absence of nonphysical field values;
    \item mesh quality, near-wall $y^+$ and a grid-sensitivity assessment of the principal integral responses;
    \item sensitivity to turbulence boundary conditions and assessment of maximum \emph{local} Mach number in the narrow passages.
\end{itemize}

More about these checks will be seen in the chapter ~\ref{ch:results}, in which some highlights of the different pursued CFD simulations results will be encountered.

\subsection*{Density convention in the force calculation}
The \texttt{forces} output function, uses \texttt{rho rhoInf} to dimensionalize the loads calculated from the incompressible solution. The active \texttt{rhoInf} values in all supplied case dictionaries are $0.0838\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ for hydrogen and $1.165\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ for nitrogen. Thus, the force values stored in the new hydrogen \texttt{forces.dat} files are already reported in newtons using the hydrogen reference density; they must \emph{not} be multiplied by density again.

The outlet kinematic pressure is prescribed as zero reference in \texttt{0/p}; this does not independently impose an absolute operating pressure. The choice of reference density and viscosity represents an assumed thermodynamic state.
